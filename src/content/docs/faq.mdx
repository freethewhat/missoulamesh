## What is MeshCore?

**MeshCore** is free, open-source software for small **LoRa** radios. LoRa is a type of radio that sends small bits of data a long way on very little power.

With MeshCore, these radios pass text messages to each other. You need no cell service and no internet.

## Why would I use MeshCore?

- **When the cell network is down.** Storms, fires, and power outages can take out cell towers. The mesh keeps working.
- **When the crowd is too big.** At a Griz game, a festival, or a big race, thousands of phones all try to use the same few towers. Texts stall and calls drop. The mesh has its own radios, so your messages still get through.
- **Where there is no signal.** Trailheads, river corridors, and back roads around the valley often have no service.
- **For fun.** You can build a network with your neighbors and see how far a message can go.

## How does it work?

You pair a small radio with your phone over Bluetooth. You type in the MeshCore app, and your radio sends the message.

**Repeaters** catch the message and send it on. A repeater is a radio, often up high on a roof or hill, that passes messages along. A message can hop through many repeaters to reach someone far away.

The first time you message someone, the mesh finds a path to them. After that, messages follow that path. This keeps the air quiet so the mesh can grow.

## What do I need?

- A MeshCore-compatible radio. Many cost about $30 to $80. See [what to buy](/hardware/).
- A phone with the MeshCore app.
- A computer with Chrome or Edge, to put the software on the radio one time.

The [join page](/start/) shows each step.

## Do I need a license?

No. MeshCore in the US uses the 915 MHz band. Anyone can use this band with no license.

## What does it cost?

Only the radio. The software is free. There is no monthly fee and no account to sign up for.

## How far can a message go?

From one handheld radio to another, often a mile or two. Hills and buildings cut that down.

A repeater up high can reach much farther, often many miles. Each repeater we add makes the mesh reach farther.

## Is it private?

**Direct messages** are encrypted. That means only you and the other person can read them.

**Public channels**, like <code>#missoula</code>, are open. Anyone who knows the channel name can read them. Do not post anything there that you would not say in public.

Some things we suggest you keep off public channels:

- Your home address or exact location
- When you are away from home
- Passwords or private channel keys
- Phone numbers
- Other people's personal details

## Can I use it in an emergency?

It can help, but do not count on it. The Missoula mesh is a small network, and a message may not get through. In an emergency, always try 911 first.

## Is MeshCore the same as Meshtastic?

No. They are two different systems, and they cannot talk to each other. See [Looking for Meshtastic?](/meshtastic/) if that is what you want.

## Can I put MeshCore on my Meshtastic radio?

Probably, yes. Most radios that run Meshtastic can run MeshCore too. Flashing MeshCore replaces Meshtastic, and you can switch back later. See [how to switch a Meshtastic radio](/meshtastic/#already-have-a-meshtastic-radio).
